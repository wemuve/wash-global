## 1. Ten cleaning habits that keep a Lusaka home fresh between deep cleans

Most homes don't need a deep clean every week. They need a few small habits that stop dirt from settling in. Here are the ones our own teams follow in their homes.

1. **Wipe the kitchen counter after every meal.** Crumbs attract ants within a day in Lusaka's warm months. A damp cloth takes thirty seconds.
2. **Sweep high-traffic areas daily.** Dust comes in fast, especially in the dry season. Focus on the entrance, kitchen and sitting room.
3. **Keep a doormat at every entrance.** Most of the dirt in a house arrives on shoes. Two mats — one outside, one inside — catch most of it.
4. **Rinse the sink after washing up.** Food residue hardens overnight and doubles your scrubbing time the next day.
5. **Air the bedrooms every morning.** Open windows for fifteen minutes while you make the beds. It clears moisture and keeps bedding fresh.
6. **Clean spills on the stove immediately.** Once oil bakes on, it needs strong chemicals to remove. Warm soapy water works while it's fresh.
7. **Squeegee or wipe bathroom tiles after showering.** This stops the white limescale marks that are so hard to remove later.
8. **Empty bins before they're full.** An overfull bin is how smells and flies start.
9. **Do a ten-minute evening tidy.** Toys, clothes and dishes back in their place. It makes the next morning calmer.
10. **Book a deep clean every few months.** Habits keep a home tidy; a professional deep clean resets it. That's when we reach behind furniture, inside ovens and along skirting boards.

## 2. How to hire a maid in Lusaka: a practical guide for families

Hiring someone to work in your home is a decision about trust as much as cleaning. Here's how to do it well.

**Decide what you actually need.** A few hours a week of cleaning and laundry is different from a live-in arrangement. Write down the tasks, the days and the hours before you start looking. Vague arrangements cause most disagreements later.

**Check references properly.** Ask for at least two previous employers and actually call them. Ask three questions: Was she reliable? Was she honest? Would you hire her again? The pause before the answer tells you as much as the answer.

**Agree pay and duties in writing.** It doesn't need to be a legal contract — a clear written note of hours, pay, transport, meals and duties protects both of you. In Lusaka, part-time maid services through a company like ours start from K150 a day.

**Consider going through a service instead.** When you hire directly, you carry all the risk: vetting, replacements when she's sick, and resolving problems. A managed service vets workers, trains them, checks their work and sends a replacement if someone can't come. You pay a little more and carry much less.

**Treat the first month as a trial.** Be clear about standards from day one, show her how you like things done, and give honest feedback early. Small corrections in week one prevent big frustrations in month three.

**Be a good employer.** Pay on time, communicate respectfully and be reasonable about meals and rest. Good workers stay with good employers — and a maid who stays for years is worth far more than saving a few kwacha.

## 3. How to prepare your home for a professional deep clean

A deep clean goes much better with a little preparation. None of this is required — our teams can work around anything — but these steps help you get the most from the visit.

**Tidy up first.** Deep cleaning means scrubbing, not organising. If floors and surfaces are covered with clothes, toys and papers, the team spends time moving things instead of cleaning. A quick tidy the evening before makes a real difference.

**Point out your priorities.** Every home has them: the oven that hasn't been cleaned in a year, the bathroom grout, the guest room before visitors arrive. Tell the team at the start and they'll plan their time around what matters to you.

**Secure valuables and fragile items.** Our teams are vetted and careful, but putting away jewellery, cash and delicate ornaments is good practice with any service — and it means nobody has to work around them.

**Plan for water and power.** Deep cleaning uses both. If there's load shedding scheduled or a water issue that day, tell us when booking and we'll plan around it or bring what we need.

**Keep children and pets in one area.** It's safer for them and faster for the team. Most families set up in one room or the garden for the duration.

**Walk through at the end.** Before the team leaves, check the rooms together. If something was missed, it's fixed on the spot — that's part of the service.

## 4. Keeping your home healthy during Lusaka's rainy season

The rains bring relief from the dust — and a new set of cleaning challenges. Here's how to stay ahead of them.

**Mould is the main enemy.** Damp walls and ceilings grow mould fast, and it affects breathing, especially for children and the elderly. Wipe down any damp patches as soon as you see them, keep rooms ventilated when the rain allows, and don't ignore a musty smell — it means mould is growing somewhere you can't see.

**Watch the entrance.** Mud gets tracked in constantly. A stiff brush at the door and a strict shoes-off habit will save your floors. Keep a mop near the entrance for the days it doesn't work.

**Dry laundry properly.** Clothes dried slowly indoors can smell musty and harbour mildew. Use the sun when it appears, space clothes out on the line, and never fold anything that's still damp.

**Check window seals and drains.** Water coming in around windows leaves stains and damages walls. Clear outside drains before the heavy rains so water flows away from the house.

**Fumigate if pests appear.** Rain drives cockroaches, ants and rodents indoors. If you see them regularly, a professional fumigation is more effective than sprays from the shop — and safer for children when done properly.

## 5. Moving house in Lusaka? Why the clean matters more than you think

Whether you're moving into a new place or handing back the keys to an old one, the state of the property decides how the move feels — and sometimes whether you get your deposit back.

**Moving out: clean like the deposit depends on it, because it does.** Landlords check ovens, bathroom fittings, inside cupboards and behind appliances. These are exactly the spots regular cleaning misses. A professional move-out clean costs a fraction of a withheld deposit.

**Moving in: don't trust a "clean" empty house.** A house that looks clean has usually had a quick sweep. Cupboards, toilets, window tracks and kitchen surfaces deserve a proper clean before your family's belongings and food go in.

**The order matters.** Clean first, then move in. Cleaning an empty house is faster, cheaper and far more thorough than cleaning around boxes and furniture. If you can only book one clean, book it for the empty house.

**Don't forget the outside.** Sweep the yard, clear the drainage and check the geyser and outside rooms. First impressions of a new home include the approach to the front door.

**Book early.** Month-end is when everyone moves. Book your clean at least a week ahead so you get the date you need.

## 6. Choosing a cleaning service in Lusaka: what to ask before you book

Not all cleaning services work the same way. A few questions up front will tell you who you're dealing with.

**"Are your cleaners vetted and trained?"** Anyone can buy a mop. Ask how workers are checked and whether they receive training. A serious company will answer this easily.

**"What exactly is included?"** "Deep clean" means different things to different companies. Ask for the task list: inside the oven? Windows? Inside cupboards? Skirting boards? If the answer is vague, the clean will be too.

**"How is the price worked out?"** Honest pricing depends on the size and condition of your home, so expect "starting from" figures with a confirmed quote after a few questions. Be cautious of a flat price quoted with no questions asked — it usually means corners cut or extras added later.

**"What happens if I'm not happy?"** A company that checks its work will have a clear answer. Ours is simple: walk through with the team before they leave, and anything missed is fixed on the spot.

**"When do I pay?"** You should pay after the work is done, not before. Pay-after-service keeps the incentive where it belongs — on doing the job well.

**"Can I see real reviews?"** Look for genuine, named reviews. Ours are on our website and our Google profile, from real customers in Lusaka.
